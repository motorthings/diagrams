SWT Experiment

Workflow Architecture

Three business workflows simulated against the same company model — auto claims, commercial underwriting, and subrogation — each exposing different seams where translation debt accumulates when AI inserts into the flow.

How the three workflows connect

Claims is the spine. Subrogation hangs off the far end of it. Underwriting runs in parallel, feeding the same agents and the same organizational context.

Primary value stream

Auto Claims

FNOL intake to closure, with two pipelines — AI for simple claims, human for moderate and complex. The most mature and most deeply modeled workflow in the simulation. It is the largest surface for AI insertion and the source of the subrogation cases downstream.

↓Claims → Subrogation. Every paid claim with recovery potential becomes a subrogation case. The critical seam of the whole experiment.
↓Underwriting runs parallel. Separate value stream, but shares agents, metrics, and the same company context.
Downstream recovery

Subrogation Recovery

Post-claim recovery from at-fault parties. Originates from a closed claim, so it inherits every upstream failure. Highest seam risk in the model — recovery depends entirely on documentation it never produced itself.

Parallel value stream

Commercial Underwriting

Application to bind. Guidewire PolicyCenter-aligned. Models the sales cycle and the broker → underwriter → actuary translation chain. AI augments risk scoring but can't do the actuarial sign-off or the quote.

The stakes of the connection: underwriting is a separate value stream, so failures there don't contaminate claims. But subrogation is downstream of everything — a sloppy settlement decision in claims destroys recovery potential weeks later, and Tommy has no standing to fix it.

Where AI inserts

AI owns the fast lane; humans own the judgment lane.

Almost every step is AI-capable, but each has a confidence threshold below which it drops to a human exception path. The pipeline routing at triage decides which lane a claim enters: simple (single vehicle, no injury, clear liability, under $10K) goes AI; everything else goes human.

AI runsFNOL intake, triage, photo damage estimation, coverage verification, liability determination, settlement under $10K, auto-approval under $5K, payment.
Human onlyComplex damage, disputed liability, negotiation, and everything over the AI confidence thresholds — where the exception paths reroute.
↦ Metrics: cycle time (handoffs + processing), cost per claim including rework, first-pass accuracy, retention, supplement request rate, exceptions raised, translation incidents, ai_decision_overridden, human_review_triggered.

The seams AI exposes

The handoffs are where the translation debt lives.

Seam risk scores every handoff in the workflow. The most expensive ones are exactly where AI collides with institutional judgment — coverage interpretation, damage-cost reality, and the informal controls humans run underneath the formal process.

Coverage verification (0.8)Policy language interpretation. Pat's expertise is a black box — AI reads exclusions literally that specialists know are never enforced.
Damage estimation (0.7)AI uses database labor rates 6-18 months stale. Adjusters mentally correct for it. When AI bypasses that, supplement requests spike.
Approval (0.7)Sanjay's review is the bottleneck. He trusts Diana's informal review implicitly — rejection rate jumps 18% when AI skips her.
Subrogation (0.9)Downstream of everything. Incomplete files and missing liability documentation compound here — the lagging indicator of every upstream failure.

Where AI augments vs replaces

AI scores the data; the smell test stays human.

AI parses applications and scores risk factors, but the tacit knowledge that keeps a book profitable — the restaurant type that burns in year two, the contractor whose subs cause every claim, the prior carrier who quietly declined to renew — lives in Nick's head. AI pricing models produce a technical price; only a human bridges it to a competitive quote.

AI augmentsApplication intake and classification, risk-factor scoring, running pricing models, generating policy documents at bind.
AI cannot replaceQuote generation (competitive positioning) and the actuarial statement of opinion — Greg won't certify an AI model he hasn't validated, and state DOI won't accept uncertified rates.
↦ Metrics: cycle time, handoffs, translation incidents, exceptions raised, written-book loss ratio, adverse selection, rate-filing delay.
4

The seams

Every translation strips context from the risk narrative.

The broker's favorable language — "well-managed" means "owner is present," not "safe risk" — is the intake seam. The pricing seam is where actuarial rigor meets market reality. And bind is where what was sold must match what the policy says, but often doesn't.

Risk assessment (0.8)The Type 2 absence: AI doesn't know to check adjacent-building risk, prior-carrier non-renewal reasons, or application fill time. AI-written risks run 4-8% worse loss ratios after 18 months.
Application intake (0.7)Broker language → underwriter language. AI prices "well-managed restaurant" the same as any restaurant — the broker-speak translation is lost.
Bind (0.7)"Full coverage" to the broker is six exclusions in the policy. AI-generated language may not match negotiated terms.
Pricing (0.6)Technical price → quoted price. Greg won't sign off on a rate that isn't actuarially justified, but the market may need a different number.

Why it's the highest seam risk

It's downstream of everything. Every upstream failure compounds here.

Subrogation starts with a paid claim it didn't handle. Recovery potential only exists if the original adjuster documented liability evidence — and AI pipeline decisions often don't include a "should we check for recovery?" flag. Tommy screens what he can see, not what should be there. His recovery rate is a lagging indicator, and he has no standing to fix the upstream problems that gut it.

Assessment (0.9)The core Type 2 absence. 12-18% of claims with recovery potential close with $0 because the system never asked who else is liable.
Upstream debtIncomplete files and missing police reports kill demand strength. Recovery rate drops 30% when files lack evidence to support the demand.
AI insertsScreening for recovery potential and assembling demand packages from structured data — but it can't do liability review or negotiation.
Carrier tacticsRachel's team knows which at-fault carriers pay fast and which fight everything. AI treating all carriers the same drops the recovery rate.
↦ Metrics: recovery_amount, recovery rate, translation incidents, exceptions raised, cases referred to legal.
6

Jurisdiction complexity

State laws vary. The legal basis is never generic.

Liability review and negotiation depend on state-specific subrogation law — comparative negligence rules, anti-subrogation doctrines, and differing recovery rights by jurisdiction. The demand package must reflect the governing state, and disputes that can't settle move to a legal referral for litigation or arbitration.

Handoff to legal (0.6):claims → legal is a domain boundary with different priorities. The slowest, least flexible seam in the model — and where recovery disputes ultimately land.
