The Mechanics — what these phases now actually do

Track B is not a narrative. From Empower onward it commits and rolls out real workflow mutations, so it pulls the same levers as Track A and the winner is decided by conditions, not construction.

A-priori seam standardization (in waves)
Target what's gappy first. Track B ranks the seams its Target Operating Model predicts are risky, standardizes them in rollout waves — and crucially re-attempts any seam it missed or that reverted. Success on each seam is design_accuracy_base × (1 − seam_tacitness): accurate on codifiable seams (schemas, thresholds, payment rails), blind on tacit ones (negotiation, liability).
Kotter Step 5 — Empower Broad-Based Action, made mechanical
Regression under change fatigue
Shallow, imposed change reverts under load. Every sprint after a wave, each completed seam rolls for reversion scaled by actual turbulence + change exhaustion — not a flat timer. Calm quarters hold; turbulent ones wash the fix off. This is the honest cost of big-bang.
Prosci Reinforcement + Kotter Consolidate & Anchor; ~70% of transformations fail to sustain

Framework Alignment — Kotter, Prosci, McKinsey

Kotter Step 1 — Create Urgency
CEO presents competitive analysis showing GEICO/Allstate AI investments. CFO models 15% cycle time improvement = annual savings. Industry trends deck distributed. Without urgency, the organization defaults to the status quo.
John Kotter, "Leading Change" (1996) — 8-step framework for organizational transformation
Kotter Step 2 — Build Guiding Coalition
Steering committee with executive sponsorship (Kathryn as VP Claims), operations (Ron), IT (Mike), and compliance (Leslie). External consultants engaged for methodology and benchmarking. AI Center of Excellence chartered. The coalition must have enough power to overcome inertia.
Kotter: "A strong guiding coalition is always needed — one with the right composition, level of trust, and shared objective."
Kotter Step 3 — Form Strategic Vision
Vision statement: "Lead the industry in AI-enabled claims processing, achieving 40% cycle time reduction and 25% cost-per-claim improvement through enterprise-wide deployment." Target operating model designed: new roles (AI Claims Supervisor, AI QA Lead, Prompt Engineer), new reporting lines (AI CoE → CIO with dotted line to Claims VP), new governance (Ethics Review Board, Decision Appeal Process).
Kotter: "A clear, compelling vision that people can see and understand."
Kotter Step 4 — Communicate the Vision
Multi-channel communication: all-hands town halls (CEO + VP Claims), intranet site ("Our AI Journey"), department briefings, FAQ document, change champion network (volunteer basis). Communication must be frequent, powerful, and two-way.
Kotter: "Transformation is impossible unless hundreds of people are willing to help, often to the point of making short-term sacrifices."
Kotter Step 5 — Empower Broad-Based Action
Obstacles removed: vendor selected (Microsoft Copilot + custom claims agent, $8M contract), new roles posted, RACI matrix updated for all claims workflows, training curriculum developed (4 modules, 8 hours), pilot group identified (200 users). The key is removing barriers, not just announcing change.
Kotter: "Remove obstacles to the vision. Change systems or structures that undermine it. Encourage risk-taking."
Kotter Steps 6-8 — Generate Wins, Consolidate, Anchor
Short-term wins: early adopter success stories, self-reported time savings.
Consolidate: Phase 1 lessons learned, Phase 2 use cases prioritized, ROI tracking dashboard live.
Anchor: AI proficiency added to performance reviews, CoE made permanent, onboarding includes AI training.
Kotter: "Plan for and create visible improvements. Consolidate gains to produce more change. Anchor new approaches in the culture."
Prosci ADKAR — Individual Change Model
Awareness (why AI?) → Desire (what's in it for me?) → Knowledge (how do I use it?) → Ability (can I do it?) → Reinforcement (is it sticking?).
Applied through training curriculum, change champion outreach, and resistance management.
Each individual must move through all five stages for change to hold.
Prosci ADKAR Model — the most widely-used change management framework in enterprise transformations

What Track B Measures

License Activation Rate
% of employees with activated AI tool licenses. Baseline 0% (sprint 1-2), pre-rollout organic growth (sprint 3-4, ~5-15%), active rollout ramp (sprint 5-8, 15-95%), steady state (sprint 9+, ~95%). Measures access, not value. SWT: "usage means people have access; absorption is different."
Training Completion Rate
% of employees who completed AI training modules. Lags license activation — training is harder to scale than license distribution. SWT: "training completion ≠ capability. People complete training and change nothing."
AI-Assisted Claims %
Claims where AI touched any step. Progressive already at 55% baseline from existing AI pipeline. Target 80% after enterprise rollout. Maps to volume, not quality — AI touch ≠ AI value.
Milestone Hit Rate
Roadmap phase completion on schedule. Phases: urgency → coalition → vision → communicate → empower → wins → consolidate → anchor. Each phase has a target sprint. Steering committee reviews milestone status monthly.
Adoption Dashboard
Weekly active users, departments onboarded, low-adoption teams, resistance flags. Reviewed by steering committee each sprint. Low-adoption teams get additional training and change champion outreach.
Budget & ROI
$15M total program budget. Consultancy ($2M), vendor contract ($8M), new roles ($150K/year each), training ($200/head × 70K). Steering committee reviews burn rate vs. plan. ROI estimates from cycle time and cost-per-claim projections.
Employee AI Sentiment
Survey-based measure of employee attitudes toward AI. Captures Desire and Reinforcement stages of ADKAR. Resistance flagged as "standard adoption curve" and addressed with additional training and communications.
Resistance Interventions
Count and type of resistance management actions. Diagnosis: "Resistance to change — standard adoption curve." Intervention: additional training + change champion outreach. Tracked per department, escalated to steering committee if persistent.

What Track B Does NOT Do

Does not deploy without a plan
AI is not deployed into existing structures without first designing the target state. The operating model is designed, then implemented — not discovered through implementation. This is a deliberate choice, not a failure: it reduces uncertainty, aligns stakeholders, and creates accountability.
Does not grant bounded authority to a single owner
Decisions flow through the steering committee, not an individual accountable owner. This ensures cross-functional alignment and prevents local optimization at the expense of enterprise coherence. The trade-off is speed.
Does not let evidence gate the next step
The roadmap determines what happens next, not sprint observations. Phases advance on schedule, not on evidence thresholds. This provides predictability for budgeting, staffing, and vendor management. The risk is advancing before the organization is ready.
Does not expose informal control points
The transformation works through formal structures — reporting lines, RACI matrices, role descriptions. Informal controls (like Diana's pre-review) remain invisible unless they surface as resistance or adoption lag. The process doesn't systematically look for them.
