I · The claim

Discovered, not designed

Stuart Winter-Tear argues the AI operating model is discovered through implementation, not designed before it. Testing that means running one company through two change processes at once. Nobody has a spare company.

Track A · organic discovery

Deploy without reorganizing

Put AI into the existing process. Watch where it breaks. Redesign from evidence you actually observed. Slow, narrow, and blind to any seam it has not yet seen fail. That blindness is the honest cost of the method.

Track B · traditional change management

Design it, then roll it out

Kotter, Prosci, McKinsey 7S. Assess up front, rank seams by risk, design the target state, roll out broadly, govern by steering committee. Fast and wide, but only as accurate as the a-priori design.

II · The instrument

Progressive Insurance, run twice

A calibrated model of Progressive: 70,000 employees, $86B in premiums, already running 55% of claims through AI. Financials from Q2 2026 filings, culture from Glassdoor, incentives from the disclosed Gainshare structure. Eleven stakeholders carry OCEAN traits, cognitive biases, and motivational drivers, because organizations are not rational optimizers. They are people protecting their metrics and repairing broken processes with knowledge they never wrote down.

Both tracks face the same world. Same hurricanes, same attrition, same seed, same claims. The only variable is how change happens.

Seam profile is the regime dial

Tacit seams are judgment-heavy and start gappy: liability calls, negotiation, settlement. A-priori design cannot template work that lives in someone's head, so this is where Track A should win.

Codifiable seams are schemas, thresholds, payment rails. Work Track B can genuinely design in advance. That is where B should win. Whether it ever does is the whole test.

The measurement that matters

Translation debt. The share of decisions where information had to be reconstructed, re-requested, or repaired at a handoff. It is the tax an organization pays when AI is inserted into a seam nobody redesigned.

III · The finding
Start here

The instrument failed its own test

Validity gate 5 asks one question: can we construct conditions where Track B wins? If not, the simulation asserts its answer instead of discovering it. Track B does not win a single cell. Including the cell built for it to win.

An instrument that cannot produce the answer you do not want is not measuring. So I traced every metric back to the state field it reads, and every state mutation back to the mechanic that writes it.

C1 · the rig

I was giving Track A a free, permanent, omniscient advantage

The headline metric is a direct function of one variable. Track A cut that variable by 40% every two sprints, targeting the worst seam in the entire workflow, whether or not A had ever observed that seam fail. Permanent. Never regressed.

step.ai_error_rate = max(0.02, step.ai_error_rate * 0.6) # unconditional

Track B's only comparable lever was smaller, probabilistic, blocked on tacit seams, and reversible. Track A was structurally incapable of losing, and no exposed knob (vendor quality, seam profile, turbulence, horizon) changed that.

The subtle part is which half was legitimate. A's fixes being durable is real, and it is the actual mechanism the whole thesis rests on. The team owns the fix because it built the fix from its own evidence. Keep that. What was rigged is that the fix was ungated, larger, and guaranteed every cycle. A is supposed to be blind to seams it has not yet watched break. The code handed it omniscience and deleted its defining constraint.

The fix gates the cut on observed failures. Durability survives, omniscience is removed, and the winner becomes conditional on the regime instead of guaranteed.

IV · What the fix revealed

Deleting a differentiator produced a real one

Trust was written by fiat

A second rig ran the opposite direction. Fixed per-track deltas moved employee trust every sprint, with a source comment stating outright they were calibrated so B would climb and A would hold flat. Two rigs pointing opposite ways do not cancel. They make two metrics worthless.

Removed, and replaced with derivation from the already-tracked positive and negative AI experience counters, identically for both tracks. What emerged: trust climbs in both tracks together, from roughly 5.0 to between 7.7 and 8.4, and lands near-equal. The gap wobbles around zero depending on seed.

That is what an honest null looks like. The change process is not what moves trust. Working AI is.

A's debt collapses at the same point twice

Sprint 8 to 11, Track A falls from roughly 15 to under 2 and stays there. Seed 42: 14.94, 10.32, 2.76, 1.34. Seed 7: 10.50, 14.09, 3.67, 1.88. B plateaus in the 8 to 17 band and never converges. The inflection is structural. It is where accumulated observation finally covers enough seams for durable fixes to compound.

B's failures are specific, not random

The traces name them. B ranks seams by a static risk score and polishes clean handoffs while broken ones leak. It fires a flat regression timer instead of doing Kotter's Consolidate and Anchor, so fnol_intake completes and reverts inside one sprint. And it repeatedly templates settlement_human, judgment work it structurally cannot template.

V · Conclusions

What holds, and what I cannot claim

Supported

  • In the tacit regime, Track A wins on outcomes. Six of seven metrics hold direction across two seeds.
  • Durability is the mechanism, not speed or breadth. A's fixes stick because the team built them from evidence it owns.
  • Trust is not the differentiator. It rises in both tracks and lands near-equal. Look at outcomes, not sentiment.
Not supported

The audit made a falsifiable prediction: once A's advantage is evidence-gated, the codifiable and calm cell should flip to a clean B win. That prediction has never been tested.

The de-rigging landed at 18:38. The validation sweep was written at 15:36 the same day. The sweep in the repo predates the fix it exists to validate. Until it is re-run, the regime map above describes code that no longer ships, and every result on this page is provisional.

OPEN   Four things still unresolved
  • Agent differentiation is thin. Only 2 of 11 modelled people behave differently across tracks. The psychological layer is doing less work than intended.
  • Attrition never fires. The mechanic exists and has never triggered in any run. Undiagnosed.
  • Two seeds is underpowered for a claim about structure versus noise. Six of seven replicating is encouraging, not conclusive.
  • Track A reaches exactly 0.00 debt at sprint 12 on seed 42. Post-fix that means A observed and repaired every seam, plausible on a small workflow over 16 sprints. It also sits close enough to the C1 failure mode to deserve a look rather than a victory lap.
What the experiment is actually for

The winner is the least interesting output. The two tracks are two ends of one axis, speed versus absorption, and the traces show exactly where each leaks. A leaks time: it cannot fix what it has not yet watched break. B leaks trust: it templates the untemplatable and its changes wash off.

A better Track A borrows B's breadth. A better Track B borrows A's ownership. The instrument earns its keep by being sharp enough to say which, and honest enough to say when it cannot.
