Executive Summary

The Experiment

A controlled simulation compared two approaches to AI transformation inside a modeled U.S. property and casualty insurer over 12 quarterly sprints. Both tracks processed the same claims with the same 11 psychologically differentiated agent profiles. The only difference was the transformation method.

  • Track A — Organic Discovery (SWT): Deploy AI into existing structures. Observe what breaks. Extract learning. Redesign one thing at a time. No reorg, no new roles, no RACI redesign.
  • Track B — Traditional Change Management (Kotter/Prosci): Build coalition. Form vision. Vendor selection. Phased rollout. Resistance management. Steering committee governance.
  • Scale: 896 total agent decisions across 180 claims per track.

What It Means

The operating model for AI is not something an organization should fully design upfront — it is discovered through implementation.

  • Track A's observe-learn-redesign loop catches and fixes meaning-loss at organizational seams. The absence detector found 32 instances of Type 2 emergence — decisions made from incomplete information where the incompleteness wasn't recognized at decision time. Track A fixed these at the seam. Track B routed equivalent findings through a steering committee.
  • Track A's higher exception rate is not a failure signal — it reflects the interruption design that makes surfacing issues safe. Track B suppresses exceptions because they're treated as escalations requiring governance review. Agents in Track B learn not to flag problems.
  • The trade-off is real: organic discovery produces better operational outcomes (lower translation debt, fewer handoff failures), but traditional change management provides more predictable governance (fewer surprises for leadership). Neither track is categorically superior — the right choice depends on whether the organization values speed of learning or predictability of process.

Why We Know

The simulation's validity rests on three design choices:

  • Psychologically differentiated agents. Decisions are made by an LLM prompted with 11 distinct psychological profiles based on real insurance-organization roles. The same agent (e.g., Diana, a skeptical senior adjuster with high power motivation) makes different decisions in Track A versus Track B because the organizational context differs, not because the agent changed.
  • Computed metrics, not hardcoded outcomes. Every metric is calculated from actual agent decisions in the decision log. Translation debt tracks translation incidents. Cost includes rework from overrides and escalations. Handoff failures count real exceptions at workflow seams.
  • Deterministic fidelity audit. Each sprint, the fidelity auditor runs checks to verify that track-specific rules are being applied (e.g., Track B's steering committee has no frontline representation; Track A's accountable owner has operating authority). Across 896 decisions, divergence patterns are consistent in direction.

Why They Differ — The Rules

Interruption Design
Track A: Pause, override, and stop are designed into the workflow. Agents exercise them without political consequence. Result: more exceptions surfaced, more supplements requested.
Track B: Exceptions are escalations — they signal failure. Governance filters before visibility. Result: fewer exceptions surfaced, but handoff failures accumulate.
Learning Extraction
Track A: After every deployment, the Discovery Engine finds patterns, proposes ONE redesign. Translation debt is addressed as it appears.
Track B: Learning goes through steering committee → roadmap phase → vendor assessment. Translation debt accumulates until the "consolidate gains" phase.
Deployment Constraint
Track A: Deploy AI into EXISTING structures. No reorg, no new roles, no RACI redesign. The operating model is discovered through implementation.
Track B: Reorg first (McKinsey 7S), train first (ADKAR), build coalition first (Kotter Step 2). The operating model is designed before implementation.
THEARI Gating
Track A: Evidence-gated. A discovery only advances when it has enough evidence sprints + confidence + measured improvement. No auto-advance.
Track B: Timeline-gated. Discoveries auto-advance after N sprints regardless of evidence. Theoretical → Empirical in 4 sprints, Empirical → Applicable in 5.
