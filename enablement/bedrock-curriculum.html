The course framework

Five cognitive shifts, not five tips

1

Recognize and articulate your quality standards

Week 0 · Recognize Your Expertise
Not:Learn to define requirements for AI.
Actually:You already know what excellent looks like. This is practicing naming it out loud.
2

Understand what complete communication requires

Week 1 · RCICE
Not:Learn a prompting formula.
Actually:All delegation — to an AI, a colleague, or a vendor — needs role, context, instructions, criteria, and examples.
3

Distinguish between teaching and testing

Week 2 · Examples & Tests
Not:Add examples to prompts.
Actually:Examples show the pattern to follow. Tests verify understanding. That is how expertise transfers.
4

Invest in inputs, not clever wording

Week 3 · Context Packs
Not:Find the magic prompt.
Actually:Quality comes from complete information. Your context pack — past work, voice, constraints, glossary — is your expertise made portable.
5

Think first-principles, not tool-specific

Week 4 · Tool Independence
Not:Learn which AI tool to use.
Actually:Understand how these systems work at the foundation level, so you can adapt to any tool today or in the future.

Capstone — Your Expertise Codified

A one-page assistant onboarding sheet: an RCICE prompt, three example input-to-ideal-output pairs, an acceptance criteria checklist, and a plan-first preamble. Peer-reviewed for clarity, criteria quality, and transferability across tools.

Not a tool course

No workflow building. No paid tools. Rotate across free tools — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, Perplexity — to keep the thinking tool-agnostic, and capture everything in a living notebook. The shift is the deliverable. The tool is just where you practice it.
