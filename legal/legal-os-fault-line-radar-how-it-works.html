The Fault-Line Radar · a process map

Legal AI reaches a firm through four layers.

The radar watches all four, then turns each issue into one plain instruction. Here is what goes in, what runs, and what comes out.

The question it answers
A forecast no one can act on is a forecast no one trusts. So the radar splits the work into two halves and is honest about which is which.
What should this firm build, govern, and buy next, given what the AI can do, what the law now requires, and what the firm can actually run?

Orders 1 and 3 are public. The radar measures them from the real record, deterministically and graded against its own hits and misses.
Orders 2 and 4 are private. The radar will not guess them from public data, because no one can predict one firm's deployment and P&L from the outside. You supply them.
The engine · three parts, one honesty contract
Each part claims only the determinism its object allows. That split is what lets the whole thing be credible and useful at once.
Part A T-rule · graded

Forecast core

The radar itself. Watches public capability and the rules that react to it, fault line by fault line.

  • Scoped to legal-duty stress and rules
  • Deterministic, evidence-cited, replayable
  • Backtested against what actually landed
Part B T-market · traced, not a call

Advisory layer

Adds an enablement view and turns the forecast into build-priority guidance. Never backtested as a prediction.

  • Reads the tool landscape and adoption
  • Renders the agenda this page walks through
  • Hands the economics to Part C
Part C T-model · scenario, not a prediction

Firm model

The AI Profit Paradox and the firm sim. Tests whether this firm's economics let it act.

  • Digital twin, driven by the firm’s signature
  • Confidence-intervaled, human-reviewed
  • Never sold as a forecast of one firm

The seam · inputs in, one answer out
For each issue the radar tracks, the advisory seam runs the checks below in order and stops at the first one that blocks. The exit on the right is the answer for that issue.

The advisory seam · five checks, first blocker wins
1
Worth acting on now?
Urgent if the law has moved or the capability is open before the rules arrive. If neither — let it sit.
else
Watch
2
Does it pay under how we bill?
Only binds where AI does the billable work. On hourly billing more AI is a net loss, so nothing pays until pricing moves.
else
Fix pricing first
3
Could the AI even do it well here?
Where the work is still mostly judgment (a tacit seam) or the tool isn’t good enough yet, don’t force it.
else
Defer
4
Are we equipped to run it?
If it pays but the firm lacks the tools, skills, or data, the blocker is readiness, not the idea.
else
Build capacity first
5
Passed every check?
Urgent, capturable, profitable, and the firm is ready. That’s the point of the whole engine.
→
Act now
One honest caveat: the pricing and readiness checks bind on issues where the firm is putting AI to work. On issues driven by the market rather than your own AI use (insuring your governance, unifying your operating model) the billable hour is not the blocker — you stand those up when they become required.
What comes out · five verdicts
The same landscape gives a fixed-fee firm and an hourly firm opposite instructions, because layer 4 is the one only they can answer. Each verdict names which layer was the real constraint.
Verdict

Verdict

The rule is the same · the firm is not
One live issue — verification, the requirement that firms prove their AI work was checked. The law has already moved, so it is urgent for both firms. The difference is layer 4.

Hourly-billing firm

Loses money on AI
−$124,000 / lawyer / yr

Putting AI to work frees hours it can’t resell, and under hourly billing freed hours are revenue gone. Every AI-use control on the board loses money until pricing changes.

Verdict: fix pricing first

Fixed-fee firm

Makes money on AI
+$7,900 / lawyer / yr

The hours AI saves are pure cost taken out, and resold capacity is new revenue. Same rule, same urgency, opposite economics.

Verdict: act now
The radar predicts nothing about one firm. It reports what is knowable from the record and asks the firm for the rest. The honesty boundary is the feature.
