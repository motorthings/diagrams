3 McKinsey Lens: The Agentic Organization & What It Means for Legal

McKinsey's April 2026 research on the "agentic organization" maps strikingly well onto large law firms. Below, their key frameworks translated to a legal context — because a law firm is just an extreme case of the same structural transformation every knowledge business is facing.

The Great AI Paradox

"Companies expect massive transformations from AI and have invested with that mindset. Yet more than 80% say they're not yet seeing impact on the bottom line."
Legal translation: Firms are buying Harvey, CoCounsel, and enterprise AI licenses. But 85% aren't tracking ROI, and only 18% measure returns. The gap between deployed tools and measured value is the defining challenge. This is exactly why the K&I JD asks for "frameworks to track AI solution performance, including time saved, cost impact, and quality metrics." The firm wants to be in the 20% that actually knows if AI is working.

Near-Zero Marginal Cost of Delivery

"Suppose the world could move toward near-zero marginal cost of delivery. How would that change what you're able to bring to customers?"
Legal translation: This is the billable hour's extinction event, in slow motion. When AI compresses due diligence from 15 hours to near-zero, the firm can't charge by the hour for it. But it can package it into a fixed-fee product at healthy margin, or offer it as a value-add that wins the higher-margin strategic work. The firms that figure out how to monetize near-zero-cost delivery — through AFAs, subscriptions, and tiered service models — will have a structural cost advantage competitors can't match.

Human Above the Loop

"Having a human above the loop suggests the human's role becomes judgment on top. You still need a human to ask, 'Do I agree with the decision the agents have come to?'"
Legal translation: This is the governance dimension in a nutshell. ABA 512 requires human oversight of AI outputs. But the sophistication gradient matters: Stage 2 governance has a human reviewing every AI output (in the loop). Stage 3-4 governance has the human reviewing only exceptions and edge cases (above the loop), with the system producing an audit trail that proves the boundary held. The K&I role builds the architecture that makes "above the loop" ethically defensible — the AAA arbitration example McKinsey cites is structurally identical to legal document review.

The Billion-Dollar Question

"If you eliminate every new software engineer, you have a very expensive model of only senior folks. Roll that forward ten years, and you're missing the next generation."
Legal translation: This is the competence crisis — identical dynamics, higher stakes. If AI automates the grunt work (due diligence, doc review, research), first-year associates never develop the pattern recognition to spot when the AI is wrong. The 700+ court cases involving AI hallucinations prove this isn't theoretical. The solution McKinsey points to — making L&D "central to the employee journey, not a sidecar" — is exactly the K&I mandate to "partner with Learning & Development to upskill lawyers and staff in AI literacy." Charlie's Maven teaching experience = direct capability to solve this.

75% of Roles Need Fundamental Reshaping

"Just about everybody in the workforce is going to need a new job description in the next two to three years. Most roles won't go away, but they'll be reshaped."
Legal translation: The law firm pyramid isn't just shrinking — it's being redefined. Junior associate → AI workflow supervisor. Senior associate → AI output validator. Partner → AI strategy director + client relationship owner. KM lawyer → knowledge graph curator. The K&I Program Manager sits at the intersection of this reshaping — designing the workflows, building the tools, and teaching the humans. This role isn't about deploying technology. It's about redesigning how 3,500 legal professionals do their work.

Change Management as Perpetual State

"Change management is no longer an episodic thing. It's a perpetual state. We'll have to get really good at being comfortable in constant change without introducing chaos."
Legal translation: The traditional law firm approach to change — form a committee, study for 18 months, roll out slowly — is dead. The K&I role's change management mandate ("build awareness and adoption programs that integrate AI into daily legal workflows," "promote a culture of experimentation, measurement, and continuous improvement") is describing perpetual change, not a one-time rollout. This is why the role requires both technology fluency AND teaching ability. You're not deploying a tool. You're building the organizational muscle for continuous adaptation.
Why McKinsey's framework matters for this interview
McKinsey is describing the same transformation every large law firm is navigating — they're just describing it at the Fortune 500 level. The concepts are directly transferable: near-zero marginal cost → the billable hour crisis, human above the loop → ABA 512 compliance architecture, the billion-dollar question → the legal competence crisis, perpetual change management → the K&I enablement mandate.

Dropping even one of these McKinsey concepts in the interview — framed in legal terms — signals that you understand the transformation at the systems level, not just the tool level. That's the difference between a candidate who "knows AI" and a candidate who understands what AI does to a professional services business model.
4 3-Year Progression: What Forward Motion Looks Like

Year 1

Foundation · Late 2026 / Early 2027
AI Adoption: Enterprise tool deployed. Usage baseline. 3-5 pilots running.
KM: Post-merger taxonomy alignment. Semantic search pilot on unified corpus.
Governance: Principles published ✓. Governance committee. Audit trail architecture designed.
Service Delivery: AI efficiency tracked per pilot. First fixed-fee AI product scoped.
Data: DMS consolidation roadmap. Vector DB PoC. Data quality audit.
People: AI literacy program. Champion network 50+. Shadow IT survey.

Year 2

Integration · 2027
AI Adoption: Embedded in 3+ core workflows. Usage >60% on target workflows.
KM: Semantic search live across unified corpus. Knowledge graph prototype.
Governance: Access controls enforce data isolation. Audit trails capture every interaction.
Service Delivery: 3+ AI-enabled fixed-fee products. AFA portfolio profitability tracked.
Data: Unified data layer operational. Real-time flows to AI tools.
People: AI proficiency in performance expectations. Role-specific advanced training.

Year 3

Early Transformation · 2028
AI Adoption: Agentic workflows in production. AI-native service lines generating revenue.
KM: Knowledge graph operational. Cross-practice AI pattern recognition. Client-specific layers.
Governance: Governance-by-design. Defensible evidence on demand. RFP differentiator.
Service Delivery: Subscription/outcome pricing. AI-enabled revenue >15% of total.
Data: Real-time knowledge graph. Proprietary data compounding. Firm-specific AI models.
People: AI-native career paths. Partner comp reflects AI capability. Culture of experimentation.
