# Claude Code Stage 3 — State Change, Not Theatre

You've got tools connected. Claude can pull your Jira tickets, search Glean, generate diagrams. That's capability. Capability is not the same as progress.

This stage is about the difference.

---

## The problem with AI that feels productive

Claude can generate a lot. Meeting summaries. Project briefs. Strategy docs. Status updates. Action item lists. It will produce clean, well-structured text on almost any topic in seconds.

That is also the trap.

Documents that sit in folders and don't change anything are not work. They are the appearance of work. And because AI makes generating them so easy, it is very easy to spend a day producing things that had no effect on anything — and feel like you got a lot done.

Stuart Winter-Tear, in his book *UNHYPED: From Hype to Hard ROI in the Age of AI*, calls this **theatre**. His framing is blunt: if nothing in your core systems actually changes after an AI interaction, the story ends there.

State change, not theatre.

That is the test. Not "did I use AI today?" Not "did I produce something?" The test is: what actually changed? What is different in the world — in a ticket, a decision, a project file, a relationship — because of the work you did?

Read UNHYPED. Follow Stuart on LinkedIn. His Substack is Unhyped AI. He is one of the clearest thinkers writing about this right now, and his work will save you from the version of AI adoption that looks impressive and produces nothing.

---

## What theatre looks like in practice

A few patterns worth recognising:

**The summary that goes nowhere.** You paste meeting notes into Claude, get a clean summary, save it to your vault. Nobody reads it. Nothing moves. You documented the meeting — you didn't advance the work.

**The doc that replaces the conversation.** Claude drafts a strategy brief. You spend an hour refining it. You share it. It lands in an inbox. Nothing happens. The brief did the work of a conversation you should have had.

**The plan that explains itself.** Detailed project documents with background, context, objectives, stakeholders, risks. Long enough to feel serious. Not connected to any system where work actually gets tracked or done.

**The output that creates more review.** You generate something quickly. Now someone has to read it, evaluate it, respond to it. You moved faster. The overall system absorbed more load. This is what Winter-Tear calls AI Translation Debt — the unpriced checking and reconciliation that stacks up downstream when you accelerate without redesigning the work.

---

## The question to ask before generating anything

Before you ask Claude to produce something, ask yourself: **what will be different when this exists?**

If the answer is "I'll have a document," that's not enough. What will change in Jira? What decision will move? What conversation will become unnecessary because the information is now findable? What action will someone take that they wouldn't have taken otherwise?

If you can't answer that, you don't need the document. You need a different action.

---

## How to generate less and change more

**Use Claude to act, not just describe.** Instead of "write a summary of this meeting," try "take these meeting notes and create the Jira tickets, update the project file, and draft the follow-up Slack message." The output is action, not a document about the meeting.

**Keep things short and connected.** A one-paragraph project update that lands in the right Jira ticket does more than a two-page status document that lives in a folder. Brevity is not laziness. It is the discipline of knowing what actually needs to exist.

**Treat your vault as a working surface, not an archive.** Files in your vault should be alive — referenced, updated, acted on. If a file hasn't moved work in two weeks, question whether it should exist at all.

**Let Claude edit down, not just add.** One of the most useful things you can ask: "Here's what I wrote — cut it to the essential and tell me what I can remove." The instinct with AI is to add. Train yourself to subtract.

---

## State change as the measure of a good session

At the end of a session with Claude, the question is not "what did I produce?" It's "what moved?"

Tickets updated. Decisions made. Docs that were unclear, now clear. A meeting that is now unnecessary because you surfaced the answer. A draft that actually went out. A search that found the thing you were looking for.

That's a good session.

A folder full of new documents is not.
