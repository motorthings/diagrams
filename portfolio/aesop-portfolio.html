Organization layer — AESOP

See every agent project in one place, prioritize by strategic value, and manage delivery on a board or a table.

01

Why the portfolio

Individual agent projects are easy to start and hard to coordinate. The Portfolio is the organization-wide view of every project moving through the pipeline — from backlog idea to operationalized agent. It answers the questions a leader actually asks: what are we building, who owns it, what is highest value, and what is stuck. Without it, fragmented parallel experimentation; with it, a coordinated program.

04

How the strategic score works

The score balances value against cost. ROI and strategic alignment push a project up; effort pushes it down. Because effort, ROI, and alignment are editable inline, re-rating a project immediately re-ranks it — the sort always reflects your latest judgment, not a stale snapshot. Use it to decide sequencing, not as absolute truth: it is a forcing function for the prioritization conversation.

05

Table vs. Board

Table
Compare & rank. Sort by strategic score, status, or department. Best when deciding what to work on next.
Board
Manage flow. Color-coded columns delineate each stage. Best when tracking how work is moving and where it piles up.
06

From idea to operationalized agent

1Capture
New ideas land in the backlog — from a discovery session, a department request, or a promoted Org Readiness action item.
2Prioritize
Rate effort, ROI, and alignment. Sort by score to decide sequencing and assign a department owner.
3Build &
track
As a project moves through Discover, Design, Build, and Evaluate, its status updates on the board so the whole org sees progress.
4Operationalize
Shipped agents reach the operationalized stage. The portfolio becomes the running record of what your organization has put into production.

Tip: Org Readiness action items promote straight into the portfolio as backlog projects — so closing an organizational gap is tracked the same way as building an agent.
