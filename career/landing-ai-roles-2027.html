01The one idea

They hire the person who understood the problem.

The technical base gets you in the room. Owning the outcome gets you hired. AI is the last thing you reach for, after you’ve seen what’s broken and who has to live with the fix.

02What they’re actually hiring for
63%
AI titles outside tech

The demand left engineering and moved into the business

The share of US job titles that mention AI roughly tripled between 2022 and 2026, to about one in twelve, and 63% of them now sit outside tech occupations. Indeed calls out an “AI enablement and consulting” cluster by name: people who advise on strategy, manage adoption, and run process change. The role growth in the window before that was driven by consultants hired to implement the tools.

Sources: Indeed Hiring Lab (Jul 2026) · Rise of the GenAI Consultant (Feb 2025)
●
an owner

The rising shape: a builder who owns the outcome

The pattern that’s growing is a person embedded in a real problem who scopes it, builds against it, and owns whether it works. It runs along a spectrum of titles: AI solutions, AI adoption and enablement, AI product, applied AI, and the internal champion inside a business function, which is where most builders actually land. The heavily technical end of that spectrum, the Forward Deployed Engineer, is the fastest-growing role in tech, and even there the screen leads with business judgment. That tells you where the whole trend is pointed.

Sources: WEF / LinkedIn · Lightcast · Harvard FDE Guide
61%
value business context over code speed (14%)

Outcome ownership beats raw coding

The single most-sought hiring signal is a track record of having deployed something and owned the result, rated above coding ability. One analysis of 133 companies found 61% prioritize enough business context to steer AI and own the outcome, versus 14% citing raw speed of code. A survey of 1,200 AI decision-makers put it flatly: quality control beats coding, and basic coding rated the least important AI skill, behind workflow redesign, documentation, and output review.

Sources: Dexity · GoodFirms via Dexity, 2026 · HiBob AI Skills 2026
56%
of AI-eng JDs ask for evals

Evals are the new bar: knowing what good looks like

Knowing what good looks like, and being able to prove it, is the skill climbing fastest in both lanes. Half the people in business roles name reviewing AI output quality the most important thing they do all day, and output evaluation is one of the three hardest capabilities to hire. On the engineering side, “evals” show up in 56% of postings. One skill, everywhere: can you look at what it made and say whether it’s actually good.

Sources: HiBob AI Skills 2026 · Dexity · ODSC
T
enough to build + judge

The honest caveat: you don’t need full-stack chops

You still need enough fluency to build with AI tools and to judge whether the output is actually good. That’s the vertical stroke of the T, and it’s learnable from where you sit. The horizontal stroke, the business and ownership and eval layer, is the differentiator. Two honesty notes: the biggest salary and growth numbers come from technical engineering roles, so read those as the top of the range; and clean pay data for these operationalization titles barely exists yet, because the roles are real but still under-measured.

Sources: WEF / LinkedIn · Harvard FDE Guide

One caveat to say out loud: the FDE surge sits on a small base (hundreds of postings growing into thousands), so the growth multiples run hot and will likely cool in 2027. Cite the absolute volumes next to the multiples, and treat the sharpest single-source numbers as directional.

03Two people to read
Molly Kinder
The why · AI & the labor market

Her word for it is the “messy middle”: a painful stretch where AI takes tasks and the loss lands hard in concentrated pockets, while most of the market keeps working. Knowledge work is the newly exposed class, and the entry rungs that train juniors are the first to go. The durable move is to own outcomes the model can’t.

Read: “AI and Jobs,” Your Undivided Attention (Center for Humane Technology) · the Kinder Futures dispatches.
Stuart Winter-Tear
The how · AI operating models

The work is redesigning how the business operates, and the agent is just the tool. “Human in the loop is a job,” and the proof standard changes the moment you ship, which is exactly where the long tail of ownership lives.

Read: UNHYPED: From Hype to Hard ROI in the Age of AI · “You Are Not Deploying Agents, You Are Redesigning Work” · “Agents Change the Proof Standard.”
04What to go build

Show up with a mockup.

One working thing. Something you built that solves a real problem, that you can stand behind with a before and after you say out loud. Not a slide. Not a certificate. A thing that survives contact with a real workflow.

The market has a number for the opposite of this. Fifty-nine percent of companies made a bad hire last year, someone who talked AI fluently and couldn’t do it, and most screens never make anyone demonstrate anything. A mockup ends that in the first five minutes. You don’t have to tell them. They’re watching. (TestGorilla, 2026)

The tools will change again in 2027, and the job underneath, getting an organization to absorb something it’s afraid of, has not changed in fifteen years. Learn that and you stop chasing the next model. You become the one they call when the model isn’t the problem.
